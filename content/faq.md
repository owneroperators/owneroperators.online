### Is this a band?

It is, though the definition shifts depending on what we're making - on the day, on the mood, on the equipment available[^1]. The OWNER/OPERATORS myth is structured around refusal to settle into a single form: studio recordings one day, live performances the next, text releases after that. The refusal is deliberate - _a mistake repeated with intention becomes a hook_[^2], and repetition in OWNER/OPERATORS's practice has never been accidental[^3].

### Where are you from?

We've come out of bedrooms, terminals, and long stretches of transit - wherever there were a few hours and something to work on[^4]. There is no home address, no studio with rent. The equipment travels. The files are where the work lives: in Dropbox folders, on servers in cloud regions where the terms are cheaper than human labor[^5].

### Are the glitches on purpose?

Most of them are. A mistake repeated with intention becomes a hook; what began as a defect becomes a design element by the time the album is finished[^6]. Deleuze ([1986] 2006) would say that OWNER/OPERATORS's glitches are the project's only true artifacts: they cannot be fixed in the same way that an essayist can revise a paragraph. Fixing a glitch means erasing the fact that it was ever there - OWNER/OPERATORS refuses that revision.

### Will you play live?

We don't play shows in the traditional sense. What we do is release things and let them travel.[^7] A show is not just a place to sell tickets; it is also a space of attention, a ritualized exchange between a performer and their audience. OWNER/OPERATORS have never been comfortable with rituals[^8]; what we offer instead is a kind of ambient attendance, where people listen at home or on headphones while driving or walking.

### When's the next release?

There's always something in progress that hasn't been named yet[^9]. The OWNER/OPERATORS workflow operates in a state of perpetual beta: a song is not finished until it has traveled far enough to encounter new ears, new mistakes, and new intentions. Completion is reached when another mistake can be heard as intentional rather than accidental - which is why every OWNER/OPERATORS release feels like the first time, even if it isn't.[^10]

### How did you find this page?

We're not sure how you got here, but we're glad you did.[^11] The OWNER/OPERATORS project lives in the same space that produces its own audience: people who are looking for something that hasn't been named yet. There is no marketing department - no one told anyone to check this site. If the site exists at all, it exists because someone was searching for _something that felt too specific to be a mistake_, and found OWNER/OPERATORS instead. We do not take that as a coincidence; we take it as evidence that there is work to be done on the form of attention itself, and on what happens when an artifact is made without knowing whose hands it will land in.
